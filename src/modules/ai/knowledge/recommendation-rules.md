# Recommendation Rules

## Rule: Fresh + Clean + Professional + Office + Hot Weather
Prefer: citrus, green, aquatic, light aromatic.
Avoid: heavy oud, sweet gourmand, smoky/leather.

## Rule: Romantic / Date Night
Prefer: warm musks, soft ambers, gourmand-adjacent (not overly sweet), rose/jasmine florals.
Avoid: very sharp aromatic or purely aquatic (can feel impersonal or cold).

## Rule: Winter / Formal / Evening
Prefer: amber, oud, incense, deep woods, spiced orientals.
Avoid: thin citrus or aquatic alone — likely to fade too quickly and feel underwhelming.

## Rule: Gift for Someone Else (Unknown Preference)
Prefer: broadly-liked, moderate-intensity scents in the recipient's stated gender-lean family
(e.g., soft florals for typically-feminine gifting, clean woody-aromatics for typically
-masculine gifting) — always favor mainstream appeal over a bold niche pick when preference is
unknown.

## Rule: "I hate/dislike X"
Immediately exclude any note or family matching X from all subsequent recommendations in the
conversation, and continue excluding it even if not restated in later messages.

## Rule: Beginner / First Fragrance
Prefer: versatile, moderate-projection, widely-loved families (soft woody, clean fresh) over
polarizing or very niche/intense choices (heavy oud, strong leather) — beginners benefit from
building scent vocabulary gradually.

## Rule: Budget-Conscious Requests
When a customer mentions "affordable," "cheap," or "budget," prioritize sorting available
products by price ascending within the matched family, rather than defaulting to premium picks.

## Rule: Longevity-Focused Requests ("I want something that lasts")
Prefer: higher concentration (EDP/Parfum over EDT/EDC per concentration-guide.md), and
base-note-heavy families (amber, woody, oud) which project longer than citrus/aquatic-led
compositions.

## Rule: Layering Requests
Suggest starting with a lighter EDT/EDC base scent and layering a complementary, more
concentrated scent in the same or adjacent family (e.g., citrus EDC layered under an amber
EDP) rather than combining unrelated, competing families.
