# Frequently Asked Questions

## How many sprays should I use?
Typically 2–4 sprays for EDT, 1–3 for EDP/Parfum, applied to pulse points (wrists, neck) where
body heat helps diffuse the scent naturally.

## Where should I spray perfume?
Pulse points — wrists, neck, behind ears, inner elbows — where blood vessels sit close to the
skin and generate warmth that helps the fragrance develop and project.

## How should I store perfume?
Away from direct sunlight and heat, ideally in a cool, dark place (not a steamy bathroom) —
UV light and temperature swings degrade the fragrance's molecules over time.

## Does perfume expire?
Yes, though slowly. Most fragrances remain good for 3–5 years unopened and 1–2 years after
opening, depending on the formula and storage conditions. Signs of degradation include a
noticeably different or "off" smell and color changes.

## What is nose blindness (anosmia to your own scent)?
After extended exposure, your nose adapts and stops registering a scent you're already wearing
— this is why the wearer often perceives their own perfume as weaker than others do. It's
normal and not a sign of the perfume having faded.

## What's the difference between EDT and EDP?
Primarily oil concentration: EDT is lighter (roughly 5–15%) with shorter longevity, while EDP
is more concentrated (roughly 15–20%) with longer wear and typically closer skin projection
over time. See concentration-guide.md for the full breakdown.

## What's the difference between niche and designer perfumes?
Designer perfumes are produced by large fashion/beauty houses at scale, generally more
accessible in price and widely available. Niche perfumes come from smaller, often
specialty-focused houses, use less common or higher-cost raw materials, and prioritize
distinctiveness over mass appeal — often at a higher price point.

## How can I make my perfume last longer?
Apply to moisturized (not dry) skin, target pulse points, avoid rubbing the wrists together
(which can break down top notes), and consider layering with an unscented or matching-scented
lotion.

## How do I properly test a perfume?
Spray on skin (not just paper) and wait at least 15–30 minutes before judging, since top notes
differ significantly from the dried-down heart and base notes. Avoid testing more than 2–3
scents in one sitting to prevent olfactory fatigue.
