# Fragrance Concentrations

## Eau de Cologne (EDC)
Oil concentration ~2–5%. Longevity: 2 hours. Projection: light. Price: usually lowest. Best
usage: quick refresh, hot climates, layering base.

## Eau de Toilette (EDT)
Oil concentration ~5–15%. Longevity: 3–5 hours. Projection: moderate. Price: mid-range. Best
usage: daytime, office, warm-weather everyday wear.

## Eau de Parfum (EDP)
Oil concentration ~15–20%. Longevity: 5–8 hours. Projection: moderate-to-strong. Price:
higher than EDT. Best usage: versatile — works for both day and evening, most popular modern
format.

## Parfum / Extrait de Parfum
Oil concentration ~20–30%+. Longevity: 8+ hours, often lasts into the next day on skin/fabric.
Projection: initially strong, settles into a close "skin scent." Price: highest. Best usage:
special occasions, cold weather, those who want maximum longevity with minimal reapplication.

## Elixir / Intense / Extreme
Modern marketing terms roughly equivalent to Parfum-strength reformulations of an existing
EDP, often with amplified sweet or resinous notes. Longevity and projection typically exceed
the base EDP version.

## Perfume Oil
Alcohol-free, oil-based. Longevity: very long, close to skin. Projection: minimal — a "skin
scent." Best usage: sensitive skin, subtle personal wear, layering.

## Key Rule
Concentration name is a *guideline*, not a guarantee — actual performance depends heavily on
the specific formula's raw materials. Always weigh concentration alongside the fragrance
family (heavier families like amber/oud/leather perform strong even at EDT strength).
